Klawonn and Smets model every belief expansion in the Transferable Belief Model as a specialization matrix that moves basic belief mass from each set to its subsets, and derive Dempster's rules from minimal commitment and commutativity rather than from probability. They prove unnormalized Dempster conditioning is the least committed specialization that makes the excluded set impossible, that the Dempsterian specialization matrices are the largest commuting family containing the conditioning matrices, that commonalities are their eigenvalues, and they sketch the dual de-specialization (retraction) and generalization (disjunctive combination) processes.
For propstore this justifies order-independent conjunctive combination over stored evidence at render time, gives retraction of a source as pointwise division in commonality space, and keeps unnormalized m(empty) as honest conflict information.
