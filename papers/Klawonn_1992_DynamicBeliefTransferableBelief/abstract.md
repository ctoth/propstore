# Abstract

## Original Text (Verbatim)

The fundamental updating process in the transferable belief model is related to the concept of specialization and can be described by a specialization matrix. The degree of belief in the truth of a proposition is a degree of *justified support*. The Principle of Minimal Commitment implies that one should never give more support to the truth of a proposition than justified. We show that Dempster's rule of conditioning corresponds essentially to the least committed specialization, and that Dempster's rule of combination results essentially from commutativity requirements. The concept of generalization, dual to the concept of specialization, is described.

---

## Our Interpretation

The paper asks why Dempster's conditioning and combination rules are the right update operators in a non-probabilistic belief-function model. Treating updates as subset-directed mass-transfer matrices, it shows conditioning is the least committed such update and combination is forced by requiring updates to commute. This gives propstore a principled, order-independent default for combining stored evidence at render time, plus a dual for disjunctive combination and a route to retraction.
