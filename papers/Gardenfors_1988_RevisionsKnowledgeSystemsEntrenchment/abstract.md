# Abstract

## Original Text (Verbatim)

When a knowledge system receives new information which is inconsistent with its current knowledge base, it must revise its knowledge set in order to preserve consistency. Also, when a piece of knowledge is retracted, other pieces of knowledge must also be withdrawn. A knowledge system thus needs mechanisms for revising and contracting the stored knowledge. This paper investigates the logic of such mechanisms. We develop two related approaches. One is based on a set of rationality postulates or integrity constraints for revisions and contractions. The other involves a notion of epistemic entrenchment which orders the knowledge according to its importance. The main result of the paper is a representation theorem which says that a revision method satisfies the set of rationality postulates if and only if there exists an ordering of epistemic entrenchment satisfying certain conditions such that this ordering determines which pieces of knowledge are withdrawn. We also prove that when a knowledge system is represented as a finite set of atomic facts together with a deductive mechanism, the amount of information needed to determine the ordering of epistemic entrenchment is linear in the number of atomic facts.

---

## Our Interpretation

The paper does not authorize free-form metadata overrides for revision. Its epistemic entrenchment is a total qualitative ordering over propositions, relative to a knowledge set, constrained by entailment and conjunction and linked by representation theorems to AGM contraction. Reliability of a source or preference for a context can influence a system only through an explicit model that produces or revises such an ordering; those labels are not themselves entrenchment keys.
